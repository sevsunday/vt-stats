VTSR-T: Combined Critique & Path Forward

A synthesis of two readings — the Cambridge-paper-grounded measurement framing and a production-systems benchmarking against Glicko-2, TrueSkill 2, PandaSkill, and CS2's dual-track. Where the two agree, the convergence is load-bearing. Where they disagree, we propose an empirical tiebreaker. Every critique below maps to a tractable fix in §7.

v3 retrospective (§8 below): the validator was built, three reforms were tested, one shipped in canonical, two were refuted. Section 8 condenses the empirical record — what was tested, what survived, and what didn't — into a single readable view.

6.1 The Validation Gap

Both critiques converge here. Highest priority. Gates every other improvement.

In modern matchmaking science (Cambridge "Skill Issues", TrueSkill 2, PandaSkill), a rating system is judged by its predictive accuracy. We have never measured whether VTSR-T predicts anything — not match outcomes, not next-match performance, not leaderboard stability.

Important caveat: reliable team-level winner data is sparse (only the clean_win subset). But that doesn't excuse skipping validation — we have something the Cambridge dataset didn't: a per-player composite performance score (P_i) for every player in every match. That's a richer validation target than win/loss.

THE FIX:

Build scripts/validate_elo.py — rank correlation, calibration, bootstrap stability, axis ablation, log-loss, and the small clean_win anchor. One JSON report. Settles every other disagreement empirically.

6.2 Median vs. MAX — Two Jobs, Different Math

A real tension. Both have valid uses. The validator picks the winner.

VTSR-T calculates expected performance ($E_i$) against the median rating ($\bar{R}_i$) of the opposing lobby. Dehpanah et al. (2021), across 100k+ matches in PUBG, LoL, and CS:GO, showed that team threat in tactical shooters is empirically dominated by the MAX rating.

The honest reading: these are two different questions. Median is right for individual expected-performance calibration (don't let one ringer deflate everyone's $E_i$). MAX is right for team-outcome prediction (which team is favored). Same rating, two jobs.

Recommended fix: ship a parallel MAX-weighted $E_i$ alongside the median version, run the validator with both, let the data decide. Empirically settle-able in days.

6.3 Time-Decay Handling for Returning Players

Match-count K decay is right; the time component is missing.

VTSR-T's match-count K-factor ($K_i$) is a coarse confidence parameter — new players get high K (~52), settled veterans get low K (12). This is structurally correct — it implements the same idea as Glicko-2's Rating Deviation, with simpler math.

What's missing: the time component. Glicko-2's $RD$ expands during inactivity, reflecting decreased confidence in a stale rating. VTSR-T treats a veteran returning after 3 years identically to one who played yesterday.

Recommended fix (smaller than full Glicko-2): add an inactivity-driven K boost on top of the existing match-count decay. K_i += min(20, 0.05 × days_inactive). Captures 80% of Glicko-2's benefit at 5% of the engineering cost. Reserve full migration for if-and-only-if this proves insufficient.

For two of the eight axes (pve_share, target_lock_pct), VTSR-T overrides the empirical running mean with a hand-tuned locked prior. PandaSkill (2025, LoL) is a modern alternative that handles role asymmetry with empirical-only role models.

The honest reading: the locks aren't math corruption — they encode an explicit normative design choice ("commanders should do PvE work, so we reward it") rather than a descriptive measurement. Every rating system embeds intent; the empirical question is whether this intent helps or hurts.

Recommended fix: one-line ablation — COMMANDER_BASELINE_LOCKED_AXES = set(), re-rate, compare leaderboards via §6.1's validator. If unlocked predicts better, ship it. If locked predicts better, the normative design is paying for itself.

6.5 EOMM & The Inflationary Rating Economy

The math is correct. The fix is a dual-track architecture — not removing the EOMM mechanics.

VTSR-T implements Engagement Optimized Matchmaking (EOMM) principles — well-studied in the literature (Chen 2017, Elmachtoub 2024, Kang 2024) and demonstrably effective at preventing churn:

  • Soft Floor Taper (1000.0): Losses approach zero at the floor, no "elo hell."
  • Loss Aversion Multiplier (0.85): Losing is mathematically dampened.

The trade-off: these mechanics break Elo's zero-sum property. Total rating across the corpus drifts upward through sustained participation, independent of skill. That's bad for matchmaking integrity (Lobby Tools' Team Balonce reads VTSR-T as a skill estimate).

Recommended fix (mirrors Counter-Strike 2): dual-track. Keep the current VTSR-T as the Display Rating (community trust). Run a parallel Hidden MMR with no soft floor and no loss aversion, used for matchmaking and validation. Same axes, two surfaces, one for each job.

7.0 Recommended Fixes & Sequencing

Top five recommendations from the combined critique, ranked by (effort × impact ÷ data dependency). The full ten-item priority list lives in §9 of elo-analysis-v2.md. Item #1 gates everything; do it first, then everything else gets easier and more justified.

1. Build the Validator

One new script: scripts/validate_elo.py. Outputs rank correlation (does pre-match $R_i$ predict post-match $P_i$ rank?), calibration plot, bootstrap leaderboard stability, axis ablation, log-loss, and the small clean_win anchor. Plus the synthetic-winner proxy (mean $P_i$) validated against clean_win — if it agrees 85%+, we sidestep the small-N winner problem entirely.

> Effort: ~1 week. Gates every other fix.

2. Locked-Priors Ablation

One-line change: COMMANDER_BASELINE_LOCKED_AXES = set(). Re-rate the corpus, compare leaderboards via #1's validator. If unlocked predicts better, ship it. If locked predicts better, the normative design is paying for itself empirically. Either way, the descriptive-vs-normative debate ends.

> Effort: 1 hour + corpus re-rate. Trivial.

3. Parallel MAX-Weighted $E_i$

Don't deprecate the median — ship a parallel MAX-weighted $E_i$ alongside it. Run #1's validator with both. Three possible outcomes: median wins for individual calibration + MAX wins for team prediction (most likely), MAX wins both, or median wins both. The data picks.

> Effort: ~1 day. High impact if MAX wins.

4. Inactivity-Driven K Boost

Smaller fix than full Glicko-2 RD migration. Add an additive boost on top of the existing match-count K decay: K_i += min(20, 0.05 × days_inactive). Captures 80% of Glicko-2's benefit at 5% of the engineering cost. Reserve full migration for if-and-only-if this proves insufficient (>50 ELO miscalibration on returning players).

> Effort: ~half day. Simple, additive.

5. Parallel Hidden MMR → Dual-Track Architecture

Mirrors Counter-Strike 2. Phase 1: emit a parallel elo_current_pure.json from a second compute_elo() pass with no soft floor and no loss-aversion multiplier — same axes, same K shape, just zero-sum. Compare predictive accuracy via #1's validator. Phase 2 (if it predicts at least as well): promote to full dual-track — Lobby Tools' Team Balonce reads from Hidden MMR, dashboard shows current VTSR-T as Display Rating. Same precedent as the v2.7 thug-only mode (parallel JSON pair + UI toggle).

# Today: single rating doing two jobs (display + matchmaking)
VTSR-T = soft_floor(ELO + EOMM_mechanics)

# Phase 1: parallel pure rating, validate predictive accuracy
VTSR-T-pure = ELO # no floor, no loss aversion, same axes

# Phase 2: dual-track surfaces (CS2 model)
DisplayRating = VTSR-T # leaderboard, profiles
HiddenMMR = VTSR-T-pure # matchmaking, validation

8.0 Empirical Results & Path Forward

The retrospective view of §7's hypotheses. The validator (item #1) shipped, three reforms (#2 priors, #3 MAX, #5 K-boost) were tested end-to-end, and the results below are what we found. Two reforms refuted; one shipped in canonical. Full per-phase reasoning lives in critique/decisions/*.md and elo-analysis-v3.md.

8.1 The Validator SHIPPED

A read-only consumer of pipeline JSON. Nine independent metrics. No winner data required.

scripts/validate_elo.py scores VTSR-T against nine independent properties. Output lands in _validation/report.{md,json} and bootstrap.json. Run from repo root via python scripts/validate_elo.py; alt-mode flags (--elo-mode unlocked|max|softmax|thugs_only) score the forensic alt JSON pairs side-by-side.

The validator does not require winner data. Eight of the nine metrics use only R_pre and P_i. The ninth (clean_win prediction + log-loss) uses the small reliable subset, but the synthetic-winner proxy validated at 93.3% means a future α > 0 blend can be tested against the full corpus.

9 metrics, in shipping order

  • [1]Spearman ρ R_pre → P_i — does pre-match rating predict in-match performance?
  • [2]Calibration MAE — do predicted E_i match observed P_i across rating-gap buckets?
  • [3]Self-consistency ρ — does past P_i predict future P_i? (ceiling for the rating)
  • [4]Bootstrap stability — 100×80% resampling → top-20 Jaccard + per-player σ
  • [5]Synthetic-winner proxy — team with higher mean P_i vs clean_win (≥85% unlocks α>0)
  • [6]clean_win prediction — mean / hard MAX / softmax MAX team aggregations scored side-by-side
  • [7]Log-loss on clean_win — can we tell 51%-confident from 95%-confident apart?
  • [8]Single-axis ablation — drop each axis, measure ranking displacement
  • [9]Dirichlet weight perturbation — 50 samples around current weights, ranking stability

8.2 Phase 1 + 2A Baseline

First validator run on canonical VTSR-T. 100 matches, 35 players, 30 clean_wins.

What worked. The composite axes are doing real work (self-consistency 0.804). Pre-match rating tracks in-match performance (Spearman 0.462 across 884 player-matches). The leaderboard is stable under match resampling. Predictions are calibrated within 2pp of observation.

What was unexpected. Predicting team wins from team mean rating fell to 43.3% on the small clean_win subset — below coin-flip, well below Cambridge's 60-64% benchmark. A follow-up Phase 2A diagnostic showed hard MAX team-aggregation lifted this to 53.3% (+10pp), in exactly the direction Dehpanah 2021 predicted. This was the smoking gun that motivated Phase 2C.

Caveat: zero of 30 clean_wins have a team R-gap > 100 ELO. The 43.3% / 53.3% numbers are measured exclusively on tightly-balanced matches. The system's true predictive ceiling on lopsided matches remains untestable on this corpus.

8.3 Phase 2B: Locked Priors Ablation REFUTED

v2 §6.2 hypothesis: hand-tuned commander priors "corrupt empirical integrity." Tested via full re-rate.

Added exclude_locked_priors=True as an alt mode in compute_elo(). Re-rated the full corpus with the two locked commander priors removed (target_lock_pct: -0.10 and pve_share: -0.05). Validator scored canonical and unlocked side-by-side.

Top single-player rating delta: 3.9 ELO. Bootstrap noise floor: 27 ELO. The locks have less effect on commander ratings than the natural noise in the rating itself. All headline validator metrics are flat across modes (Spearman 0.4623 / 0.4623; self-consistency 0.804 / 0.806; synthetic-winner 93.3% / 93.3%).

Decision: keep canonical locks. Data did not show the locks materially affect anything; choice now reduces to a values question (descriptive empirical vs. normative design intent). The forensic alt JSON pair (elo_current_unlocked.json) is retained for re-evaluation at corpus n > 200.

8.4 Phase 2B: Inactivity K-Boost SHIPPED IN CANONICAL

v2 §7.1 compromise (additive K-boost vs full Glicko-2). The only canonical algorithm change since v2.

Modified k_factor() to add +min(20, 0.05 × days_inactive) on top of the existing matches-played K. compute_elo() now tracks each player's last-match date through the chronological loop and threads days_inactive into every K-factor call.

On dense data (everyone plays close together) the boost is essentially zero. The mechanism is built to absorb returning-player uncertainty when somebody actually disappears for months — without inflating volatility on regular players. Validator drift pre vs post: identical to four decimal places. No regressions.

No PIPELINE_VERSION / ELO_SCHEMA_VERSION bump required. New constants (K_INACTIVITY_BOOST_RATE, K_INACTIVITY_BOOST_MAX) surface as additive sentinels on elo_current.json; existing readers unaffected.

8.5 Phase 2C: MAX vs Median REFUTED CATASTROPHICALLY

v2 §6.1 hypothesis (motivated by Dehpanah 2021 + Phase 2A's +10pp preview): swap compute_elo's median(R_others) reference to max(R_others) or softmax-weighted MAX.

Added expected_performance_mode parameter to compute_elo() supporting median / hard_max / softmax_max. Pipeline emitted two forensic alt JSON pairs (elo_current_max.json, elo_current_softmax.json). Each is a complete corpus re-rate, not a post-processing reweight.

Hard MAX collapsed. Spearman ρ fell from 0.462 to 0.188. Mean rating drifted +522 ELO above the 1500 anchor. Bootstrap proxy std nearly doubled (27 → 50 ELO). VTrider, the canonical #1, dropped out of the top 5 entirely. Softmax MAX is a less-pathological version of the same failure mode — still strictly worse than canonical on every headline metric.

Why it broke. When E_i is computed against max(opponents), every sub-max player has their reference dragged toward the lobby ceiling. They are expected to do much worse than they actually do, so the rating math gives them positive points every match. Result: systematic upward drift, not skill-tracking.

What survives. Phase 2A's +10pp finding for post-hoc team aggregation at lobby-formation time is still real and useful for downstream consumers like Lobby Tools' Team Balonce. Different question, different answer. See §8.6.
