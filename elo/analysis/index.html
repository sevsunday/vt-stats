VTSR v4: Both Sides of the Coin

A performance-based thug rating and an outcome-based commander rating, re-examined at twice v3's corpus. Should the ratings be split by faction, by role, or by ship? Both sides argued, with the industry's own record and the corpus's own numbers.

Drafted with an AI assistant, as the previous three were. Every number on this page comes from python scripts/validate_elo.py (validator v1.8, §1–§20) run on 2026-10-04, and anyone with the repository can regenerate all of them. Every design change is governed by a rule written before the numbers that judge it existed. Several proposals the tooling itself drafted have been tested under those rules and refuted (§3). Bracketed numbers are source citations; the full list is §15.

Quick answers

The questions the community actually asks, in plain words. Every answer links to the numbered section that carries the numbers, and to the ELO page where the mechanics are explained step by step.

Why don’t 1v1s and 2v2s count?
VTSR-T scores you against the room you played in. With two players in the room every stat comes out at exactly ±0.5 no matter what happened, and with four the spread is noise — the math has nothing to measure. The games are not worthless: the better-rated side won 10 of the 15 we have a result for. So the plan is a separate small-format record first, and a small-format ladder once there are enough results — the way StarCraft II, chess and Counter-Strike’s Wingman each handle a different format.
Details: §10 Include all games? §13 Roadmap, item 6
Why isn’t win/loss part of VTSR-T?
It is already computed — a win/loss ladder runs beside the performance rating on every rated match — but its weight in the published number is zero. Twice it has been tested as a blend and twice held, because it did not predict matches any better and a team result is a weak signal about one player in a ten-player game. It will be re-tested now that there are 170 determined matches instead of 39. The commander rating, VTSR-C, is pure win/loss already.
Details: §5.1 Performance vs wins §13 Roadmap, item 2 ELO · How it works
Why did my rating drop when we got stomped, or when my commander fed?
Because you are measured against everyone in the lobby, both teams, and a stomped team scores below the room on almost every axis. Your team’s result explains about a fifth of your per-match score; a third of the match spent on foot at base waiting for a ship averages −11 on its own. Strong thugs on a losing team still out-perform the room on average — and still lose rating 76% of the time, because the bar their rating set was higher. The fix on the roadmap is to extend the ship-denied-time credit to every tier.
Details: §5.2 Team result §5.3 Time on foot §13 Roadmap, item 5 ELO · Commanders & fairness
Why not StarCraft-style faction ratings? Is Hadean overpowered?
Hadean does win more than its commanders’ and thugs’ ratings explain — about 62 commander-rating points’ worth — and its pick share has roughly tripled in a year. But StarCraft’s per-race MMR works because it is 1v1, you pick your own race and there are millions of players; here the commander picks the faction for the whole team and there are 45 rated players, so three ladders would leave most people provisional forever. What transfers is the chess idea that the matchup itself carries an edge: one faction term in the commander ladder’s prediction, frozen at +60 Hadean and judged only on games played after this document.
Details: §7 Faction-based ELO §5.6 Faction is not neutral §13 Roadmap, item 1
Why isn’t there a support / utility rating?
Every role rating that lasted has the role declared before the match (Overwatch); every one that guessed the role from stats died (Dota 2’s Core/Support split in seven months, Riot’s positional ranks after one preview). Here your role is whatever ship and weapon the commander handed you, and a role the system has to guess is a role players can pose for. The data also says the premise is half right: PvE work is already rewarded — the winning team has more of it 79% of the time. What is over-rewarded is clean fighting (accuracy, efficiency), and that is a weights fix, not a new ladder.
Details: §8 Role-based ELO §5.4 Two axes point the wrong way ELO · The 8 axes
Does commanding punish my rating?
On average the opposite: commander-matches gain +2.1 versus +0.9 for thug-matches, because the commander adjustment lowers the bar. The catch is that the bar is set to the average commander, so strong fighters harvest it (Muffin +14.5 a game in the chair) while a few still bleed (Lithium −4.0). Measuring each commander against their own thug numbers instead is roadmap item 3. Your win/loss record as commander lives in a separate rating, VTSR-C, where your thug rating is not touched.
Details: §5.5 Who commands §8 Role-based ELO §13 Roadmap, item 3 ELO · Commanders & fairness
Why no per-ship rating?
The commander builds your ship, it changes several times a match, and per-player-per-ship samples are far thinner than even per-faction ones. The one study that looked found champion-specific skill mattered in League of Legends and added little in Dota 2. Accuracy is already normalized per weapon. The verdict: the Loadout tab shows your per-ship record, and it never becomes a ladder.
Details: §9 By-ship ELO
What is the difference between VTSR-T and VTSR-C?
VTSR-T (thug) is a performance rating: eight in-match statistics scored against the lobby you played in, so it moves on how you played, not only on who won. VTSR-C (commander) is a classic win/loss rating between the two commanders, with a handicap for how strong each side’s thugs were. Two ladders is the genre’s own answer — Allegiance and Natural Selection 2 both split the commander from the field player — and it is why taking the chair does not touch your thug rating.
Details: §1 Where we are §6 Why the design is defensible ELO · How it works ELO · VTSR-C ladder
Why doesn’t the site just balance the teams for us?
With 20–25 active players, volunteer commanders and hand-split teams there is nothing to matchmake; the ratings can only describe the lobby honestly. The Tools page’s Balonce Meter does exactly that — it prints the win probability the commander ladder assigns to any split, using the same formula that calls 65.4% of 675 duels. The research also says perfectly fair matches are not even the engagement optimum, and that paying people to take the unpopular role fades within weeks.
Details: §11 Matchmaking Tools · Team Balonce
Is this real statistics, or just what an AI said?
Every mechanism in VTSR is in use somewhere that pays people to get it right — chess Elo, Glicko, Microsoft’s TrueSkill 2, HLTV, baseball WAR, Bradley–Terry — and §2 names the source for each one, row by row. The AI drafted documents and proposals; those proposals went through the same written-in-advance test as anyone else’s, and most of the ones it drafted lost. Nothing changes a published rating without passing a rule written before the data existed.

What is actually going to change?
Only what passes its pre-registered rule. Queued: the faction term in the commander ladder (needs 60 more duels), a fresh test of blending win/loss into VTSR-T (170 matches now against 39 last time), and three what-if versions of the thug rating — the commander adjustment against your own baseline, re-weighting away from the two wrong-way axes, ship-denied-time credit for all tiers — plus a small-format record. Nothing on the do-not-do list is coming back.

3. How a number becomes a rule

Hypothesis → a rule written in a memo before any number exists → a forensic what-if rating file beside the real one → the validator scores both → PROMOTE / HOLD / DISCARD → only PROMOTE changes a published rating. Every proposal since v2 has gone through it. [79]

Shipped
  • Inactivity K-boost (v2 §7.1)
  • Row-exclusion gates v2.5–v2.10
  • Wins-ladder machinery (inert at α=0)
  • F9bomber ledger import
  • VTSR-C economy machinery (inert at αc=1)
  • Ladder eligibility + 90-day inactivity (display)
  • Balonce Meter (the validated VTSR-C formula)
Held
  • ALPHA wins blend — twice
  • Rank-based lobby scoring — three times (June review)
  • VTSR-C αc economy scoring
  • Commander VTSR-T in the handicap
  • Phase 6 faction term (this document; not yet testable)
Refuted / stopped
  • Hard-MAX opponent reference (v2 critique) — ρ 0.46 → 0.19, +522 drift
  • Softmax opponent reference
  • Unlocked commander priors (v2 critique) — below noise floor
  • Converged re-rating
  • Softmax / MAX thug handicap — −2.5 to −11.7 pp

Several held and refuted entries were drafted by automated reviewers — the v2 production-systems critique, the June 2026 review, and this document's own exploratory faction fit — and went through the same gate as a proposal from the developer. The data overrules the tooling. That is the point of the gate.

The industry has not converged either. Valve split Dota 2 into Core/Support MMR in August 2019 [38] and merged it into one rating with per-role handicaps in March 2020 [40]. Riot previewed positional ranks and ended the preview after satisfaction fell 20–30 points [42]. Blizzard kept per-role SR in Overwatch because the role is locked before the match [44] and added per-race MMR to StarCraft II six years after launch [36]. NS2's developers said separate team and commander skills were “scrapped because there is no good way to implement any of it” in 2016 [61] and shipped exactly that in 2020 [58].

5. The complaint, quantified

The community's reading: VTSR-T is a raw performance meter that punishes strong thugs for what their commander or team did. Six findings, each a validator section. [77]

5.1 Performance ladder vs wins ladder (§15)

Across the 24 players with ≥ 20 matches, the published composite (thug_elo) and the inert win/loss ladder (wins_elo) correlate at Spearman 0.381. Points above the line are rated higher by performance than by wins: Domakus +258 (37–37), VTrider +242, Cloaket +237 (16–34). Below it: judgeguns, Totokomo, dd and DraconisMarch at −60 to −70.

Both readings are partly right: a strong player on balanced teams should go 37–37, and the composite does under-credit something the winners-who-don't-perform are doing (5.4). Commander share barely correlates with the gap (0.16). This is basketball's box-score-vs-plus-minus argument [18][16][17].

5.2 Your team's result moves your rating (§16)

1,084 determined thug rows. Winners: mean P +0.136, Δ +7.6. Losers: −0.096, −5.6. Only 27% of winning thugs lose rating; only 24% of losing thugs gain. Win/loss alone explains η² = 0.21 of thug P variance.

Thugs rated ≥ 1650 on a losing team still out-perform the lobby on average (P +0.098) and still lose rating 76% of the time (−6.5), because their expectation was higher. The rating does not fine a strong thug for losing; it fines them for not beating the room by as much as their rating said they would.

5.3 Time on foot at base costs rating (§17)

Thug rows banded by the share of the match spent on foot inside their own base — waiting for a ship. Under 5%: Δ +9.0, team win share 79%. Over 30%: Δ −11.4, win share 13%. Spearman(share, Δ) = −0.48. The gradient survives inside losses (−2.0 at <5% vs −12.7 at ≥30%).

Confound, stated plainly: ships go unreplaced more often on losing teams. What this establishes is that ship denial costs rating, and that the low-tier lift (below 1460) does not reach most of these rows — the worst band's mean pre-match rating is 1499.

5.4 Two core axes point the wrong way (§11)

Across 170 determined matches: how often does the winning team have the higher team-mean on each axis? Net damage share and mobility 88%, kill rate 86%, PvE share 79% — but efficiency 39% and accuracy 39%, and those two carry 31% of the composite weight.

Winners shoot structures and AI (dilutes accuracy); losers get more kills per damage fighting through repairs. Whatever the cause, this is the honest version of the utility-vs-hunter debate: PvE work is already rewarded; the axes rewarding clean fighting over effective fighting are the ones that disagree with winning.

5.5 Who commands, and what commanding does to a rating (§18)

Commanders sit at the 48th percentile of their lobby by pre-match VTSR-T (median 0.44) and below their own thug mean 52% of the time — the middle, not the weakest. 33 distinct commanders; the top four (Sev 42, Lithium 36, F9bomber 34, Darkvale 32) hold 37% of commander rows.

Commander rows gain +2.07 per match vs +0.93 for thug rows. The v2.4 adjustment is set to the average commander's shortfall, so strong fighters harvest it (Muffin +14.5, blue +11.3, Snake +8.4) while Lithium (−4.0) and DraconisMarch (−5.6) still bleed — the known failure mode of a population-baseline positional adjustment [19][21].

5.6 Faction is not neutral (§19)

Raw: Hadean beats ISDF 64.8% (79–43) and Scion 57.9% (62–45); Scion vs ISDF 49.2%. Controlling for both commanders' VTSR-C and both squads' mean VTSR-T (the canonical expected score as a fixed logistic offset), Hadean-vs-ISDF is +0.357 logit (z 2.27, p 0.023) ≈ +62 rating points; Scion-vs-ISDF −0.010. LR χ² 6.44 (p 0.040); log-loss 0.6179 → 0.6099; accuracy unchanged. Same sign in telemetry (+0.31) and F9 (+0.35). Hadean's pick share has gone from ~10% of sides in late 2025 to 37–38% — which is why this is treated as discovery and the frozen +60 candidate is judged only on games after 2026-10-04 [79].

6. Why the current design is defensible

It measures something persistent

Split-half self-consistency 0.888. Rating from per-match statistics is the move that lifted Halo 5 prediction from 52% to 68% [9].

It is calibrated

Predicted vs observed lobby-relative performance agree within a percentage point (MAE 0.009) — what lets the Balonce Meter print a probability.

It predicts, and improved with data

58.6% winner prediction with almost no lopsided games; the Cambridge benchmark's best win/loss-only system reached 64.1% on ten thousand pro matches [31]. VTSR-C's 65.4% over 675 duels is above it.

Two ladders is the genre's own answer

AllegSkill: “separate skill ratings for commanders and pilots” [50]. Hive 3.0: a high-ranked player in the chair “will no longer be expected to carry to avoid losing skill” [58].

The handicap is validated

λ = 1.0 ablated against 0, 0.5, 1.5: most accurate of the four (65.4% vs 63.9% at λ = 0).

The exclusion gates do real work

Spectators, partials, idlers omitted rather than penalized — TrueSkill 2's instinct too [9]. Top-20 Jaccard 0.83 under resampling.

The wrong-way axes are visible because it measures them

A pure W/L ladder would never have shown that accuracy anti-correlates with winning; a composite with a validator does, and can be re-weighted under a rule.

The process says no to its authors

The refuted list is longer than the shipped list. A system that mostly says no behaves like a measurement system should.

7. Faction-based ELO

The case for

Faction asymmetry is real here (5.6); a rating that ignores it mis-prices every mixed lobby by ~60 points. Skill is partly faction-specific. Blizzard added per-race MMR to StarCraft II in 2016 because “many people want to experience the ladder as other races without affecting the rank and MMR of their ‘main’ race,” seeding off-races from the main race with high uncertainty [36][37]. Aligulac rates pro SC2 per matchup, constrained so the overall rating is the mean [48]. NS2 shipped per-team skill for truly asymmetric sides [58].

The case against

SC2 is 1v1, the player picks their own race, the population is millions. Here the commander picks the faction for the whole team and there are 45 rated players: splitting them three ways gives 111 player-faction cells with a median of 8 matches; 26 reach the 25-match ranked bar; 11 players have ≥ 10 on all three factions. Small split pools are exactly where Glickman warns ratings drift [6]. And VTSR-T is lobby-relative — both teams' faction effects sit in the same lobby and cancel — so a faction axis there measures nothing and charges thugs for a commander's choice [81].

Designs, ranked
  1. Three per-player ladders (SC2 style) — rejected on the arithmetic above; the precedent does not transfer.
  2. Per-player faction offset (NS2 “average skill + team bias” [58][62], Aligulac's mean-constrained matchup ratings [48]) — the right design if per-player faction skill is ever wanted; viable later, display first.
  3. A global faction-advantage term in the VTSR-C expected score — the recommendation and the only thing v4 pre-registers: the Bradley–Terry order effect [3], applied to the matchup, not to either player. Frozen at +60 Hadean / 0 Scion / 0 ISDF; judged on ≥ 60 confirmation duels after 2026-10-04 by out-of-sample log-loss, accuracy and sign stability [79]. Verdict today: not yet testable.
  4. A faction axis or shift in VTSR-T — rejected; lobby-relative scoring makes it meaningless.

Where both sides agree: the faction matchup table and the pick-share trend belong on the Meta tab regardless of any rating decision.

8. Role-based ELO

The case for

Thugs do different jobs; a kills-and-damage composite rates the hunter above the utility player even when the utility player wins more. Dota 2 [38], League [41] and Overwatch [44] all built role-aware ratings; PandaSkill models every League role independently [27]; skill decomposes into player, hero and player-hero components [28]. Our own commander adjustment is a role adjustment, and 5.5 shows it is miscalibrated.

The case against

Every role rating that lasted has the role declared and locked before the match. Overwatch's survived for that reason [44][45]. Dota 2's Core/Support split lasted seven months before Valve “replace[d] the Core/Support separation with a single rank” [40]. Riot's positional ranks lasted one preview: players “frustrated with the grindiness, low satisfaction of off-position games, and teammates not taking off-position games seriously”; satisfaction down 20–30% [42]. In BZCC the role is whatever ship and weapon the commander handed you — a burst-gun Xypos goes to pools, a slicer Xypos hunts [80]. A role the system has to guess is a role a player can choose to flatter their rating, and a rating you can game stops measuring [70][71][73][13]. The data also says the premise is half right: PvE work is already rewarded (79%); what is over-rewarded is clean fighting (5.4) — a weights question, not a ladder question.

Designs, ranked
  1. Separate inferred-role ladders — rejected: gameable, fluid roles, the Riot precedent.
  2. Role as a declared pre-match input — not available in this game's telemetry or lobby.
  3. Recalibrate the commander adjustment to the player's own thug baseline — the WAR analogy made honest [19]; Phase 7(a) forensic alt mode.
  4. Re-weight the composite toward win-agreeing axes — off efficiency and accuracy; Phase 7(b), judged on future matches so the weights are not fitted to the games that judge them.
  5. Ship-denied-time adjustment for all tiers — generalize the v2.8 lift; Phase 7(c).
  6. Display-only playstyle descriptor (pools / hunter / mixed) — vocabulary, no rating consequence.

9. By-ship ELO

For

Ships are where skill shows; champion-specific skill was a significant predictor in League of Legends [28].

Against

The same study found it added little in Dota 2 [28]. The commander builds the ship; it changes several times a match; per-player-ship cells are far thinner than per-faction. Accuracy is already weapon-normalized.

Verdict

Display-only ship profile (already the Loadout tab); ship-normalized axis baselines as a possible future input; never a ladder.

10. Include all games regardless of size?

A match rates today at ≥ 6 real players and ≥ 240 s — 3v3 and up. The proposal: drop the size gate. The author's position: 1v1 and 2v2 change the game too much. Validator §20 tests both. [77]

What is left out. 23 small-format games (1v1 ×12, 2v2 ×7, four lopsided 2v1s), 15 with a determined winner; 14 of the 23 were played in the three weeks before this was written. The dynamics do change: median duration 1295 s vs 968 s; kills per player-minute 0.42 vs 0.31; damage per player-minute 2754 vs 2206; PvE share 0.68 vs 0.60; deaths and pools unchanged. The author's premise holds — in exactly the axes the composite weights.

But the ratings transfer, partly. Reconstructing pre-match ratings, the higher team-mean VTSR-T side won 10 of 15 (the same neighbourhood as its 58.6% on full games); the higher-rated commander won 8 of 15. A 1v1 is a fighting duel far more than a command duel.

The hard constraint is math, not taste. VTSR-T's Pi is a lobby z-score: with two rated rows every axis is ±0.5 by construction (why the v14 gate counts real rows — a 1v1 with a camera-pod gallery used to rate), with four the σ is noise. The composite cannot score these games. The wins ladder and VTSR-C are size-agnostic, so “include” is a real option for them.

The industry's answer to a different format is a separate ladder, never a mix: StarCraft II places per mode, 1v1 / 2v2 / 3v3 / 4v4 [83][84]; FIDE split rapid and blitz in 2012 because “players have different levels of play depending on the speed of the game” [85]; TrueSkill 2 treats other modes as correlated-but-distinct [9]; Overwatch 2 keeps Open and Role Queue apart [47]; CS2 keeps Wingman on its own rank [86]. F9bomber's ledger dropped its 1v1/2v2 rows at import too [79].

For including

+12% corpus, 15 more known results; real, long, combat-heavy games; the players in them (HappyOtter, DraconisMarch, Maverick, sponge in nine of fifteen) are the mid/low-tier regulars who already have the fewest rated rows; TrueSkill-family systems rate any team size [8][10]; thug skill demonstrably carries over.

Against mixing

Composite undefined below ~6 rows; dynamics differ in the weighted axes; VTSR-C's expectation assumes a squad; every precedent separates formats; commander ratings predicted these games at coin-flip.

Designs, ranked
  1. Rate everything in the published VTSR-T — rejected on the mathematics.
  2. Small games into the wins ladder RW only — mechanically sound; invisible at α = 0; cheap forensic alt mode. Phase 7(d).
  3. 1v1s as VTSR-C duels — hold: 8/15 says the ladder would learn the wrong skill.
  4. Separate small-format record, then ladder — the SC2 / FIDE / Wingman pattern; display-only now, rated at ≥ 30 determined small games with a rule written first.
  5. TrueSkill-2-style cross-mode prior — the principled long-run version of (4).

Where the author's argument lands: “too different” is supported as different enough to rate separately, not as too different to count. Do not throw the games away; do not mix them into a room-relative composite; record them separately first.

11. Matchmaking

Twenty to twenty-five active players, commanders volunteer, teams split by hand [80]. No rating design fixes that; it can only describe it honestly.

  • Who commands: mid-lobby players by VTSR-T (5.5). The weakest-player story is not supported; the concentrated-load story is. The volunteer's dilemma is the textbook description [69].
  • Records bend under the load: the two most frequent commanders hold the two lowest VTSR-C (Lithium 1289 over 135 duels, Darkvale 1311 over 107); F9bomber's ledger records Lithium 26–77 as commander [76]. Skill or burden, a W/L ladder cannot tell — the argument for displaying games commanded beside the rating.
  • Fairness is not the only objective: equal-skill matching is a special case of engagement optimization that “rarely holds in reality” [65]; optimal matchmaking under losing-streak churn differs from skill-based by 4–6% engagement [66]; slightly weaker opponents reduce churn more than fair matches do [67].
  • Bribing people into the unpopular role fades: Overwatch's Priority Pass “had a positive effect for several weeks, but its influence quickly faded,” and loot-box / XP rewards for tanking could not “put a dent in the demand for playing DPS” [47]. Make commanding measured fairly and visibly credited; do not pay for it with thug rating.
  • Closed pools drift: Glickman's committee tracks a fixed cohort's mean rating [7]; the validator's mean_vtsr history (1532 → 1543) is the same instrument here.

12. The FPS+RTS lens

Battlezone (1998) combined “a tank simulation game, a first-person shooter and a real-time strategy game” [56]; Battlezone II (1999, remastered 2018) kept the formula [57]. The lineage runs through Allegiance (Microsoft Research, 2000) [52], Natural Selection (2002) [53], Savage (2003) [54], Nuclear Dawn (2011) [55], NS2, Eximius [64] and Silica [63]. Two shipped public skill systems — AllegSkill [50] and Hive [58] — and both separate the commander from the field player.

What makes the genre hard to rate is not that it has two jobs; it is that they are coupled. A thug's measurable output depends on the commander's supply (5.3), and the commander's win depends on the thugs' fighting. Every thug is on a team whose coach controls their equipment mid-game — which is why the composite-vs-impact debate maps so cleanly onto VTSR-T vs the wins ladder [16][17].

Thug skill is FPS skill and is measurable per match — the statistics TrueSkill 2 found predictive in Halo [9] and HLTV built a rating from [23]. Commander skill is RTS skill: economy, timing, build order. RTS expertise is detectable from telemetry [32], and adding a macro-economic Spending Quotient lifted StarCraft II league prediction from 47% to 62% [33] — the long-run case for VTSR-C's economy axes, recorded, not yet scored, judged by a confirmation sample [79].

14. History: what v2 proposed and v3 found

Condensed from the v3 page (full text in critique/analysis-archive/elo-analysis-v3.md [78]). Two reforms refuted, one shipped.

Locked commander priors — refuted

Re-rating with the two hand-tuned priors unlocked moved the most-affected player 3.9 ELO against a 27 ELO bootstrap noise floor. Kept canonical.

MAX vs median opponent reference — refuted

Hard MAX inflated the mean rating by +522, halved Spearman ρ (0.46 → 0.19) and doubled bootstrap noise. Median kept. Inactivity K-boost (+min(20, 0.05·days)) shipped the same phase.

16. Author's perspective

I built this system, I command in it more than anyone else in the corpus, and I am rated by it the same way everyone else is. My own rows are in the tables above: forty-two commander matches at +3.3 a game, sixty-eight thug matches at −0.7, a thug rating 98 points above my win ladder, a commander rating that sits in the middle of the pack. I did not pick those numbers and I cannot move them except by playing.

The complaint is correct about the thing it is complaining about and wrong about the cause. VTSR-T is a performance rating. It was built to be one, and the data shows it behaving exactly like one: stable, calibrated, and disagreeing with winning in the specific way performance ratings always have — the way PER disagrees with plus-minus and HLTV disagrees with who lifted the trophy. When a strong thug loses rating on a lost game while out-performing the lobby, that is the system saying their expectation was higher than what they delivered against the room they were in. Whether that is the rating the community wants on the front page is a product question, and a fair one. It is not evidence that the math is unsound.

What the complaint gets right is that two of the eight axes are rewarding the wrong thing, and that time on foot at base is being charged to the wrong account. Those are the two findings I would act on first, and both came out of looking rather than out of the argument.

On factions: I was skeptical going in. The June position — meta property, not player property — still holds for VTSR-T. But the regression surprised me. A sixty-point Hadean effect that survives controlling for both commanders and both squads, with the same sign in two independent record sets, is not something I can call noise, and the honest response is the one in the memo: freeze a number, wait for games that have not been played, and let them decide. If the confirmation sample says I am wrong, the memo says so too.

On roles and ships: no. Not because the people proposing them are wrong about what they are seeing, but because every version of a role rating that worked anywhere had the role declared before the match, and ours is a ship someone else built. The right fix for the commander adjustment is to measure a commander against their own thug numbers instead of the average commander's. That is an alt mode and a rule, like everything else.

Every mechanism in this system is in use somewhere that pays people to get it right, and the two parts that are local inventions are the parts under test. Where the professionals disagree with each other — and on roles and factions they disagree with themselves across a single year — the only defensible position is to run the experiment with the rule written first. The memos have said no to me more often than yes, and several of the things they said no to were drafted by the tooling I am accused of trusting too much. The tooling does not get a vote. The validator does.
