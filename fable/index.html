FABLE / VTSR-T ANALYSIS
Verdict Pipeline Critique Improvements Other feedback Appendix
Independent review · incoming expert of record

VTSR-T is a superb measurement system wearing the badge of a rating system it hasn't earned yet.

v2.10 · ELO schema 10 · corpus 107 rated / 35 players · fresh validator run 2026-06-10 · prior reviewer: critique/elo-analysis-v3.md

The good is genuinely excellent. The instrumentation layer — full-rate telemetry, weapon-normalized accuracy, assist-aware kill credit, pilot-victim and self-damage carve-outs, pure-omission gates — is more careful than most published academic work in this space. The composite it produces is demonstrably real: a player's first-half career performance predicts their second-half at Spearman ρ = 0.814. The validation culture (a nine-metric validator, decision memos, forensic alt-modes) is exemplary for a hobby project and better than many commercial ones.

The uncomfortable finding is equally clear. On the freshest data, VTSR-T predicts in-match relative performance well (ρ = 0.445, calibration MAE 0.017) and match outcomes not at all: 37.5% accuracy on clean-win matches — below coin-flip — and the mid-gap bucket where rating differences should start mattering sits at 26.7%. Meanwhile the synthetic-winner proxy just hit 100% agreement, which I will argue is evidence of circularity, not validation. VTSR-T today is best described as a well-built performance index with momentum, not yet a predictive skill rating. The single highest-leverage fix is not in the math at all — it's recording the actual match outcome at the source.

Part 1

The pipeline, end to end — with every magic number on trial

Ten stages from a gzip-compressed protobuf on a player's disk to a four-digit number on the leaderboard. Each stage card lists the embedded design decisions, what value was chosen, why that value — and a verdict: principled (derived from evidence or first principles), defensible (arbitrary but reasonable; sensitivity unknown), questionable (I would change it or demand a test).

About that "one minute at a time" impression. It's actually three different cadences stacked, and none of them is a minute. The in-game collector records every lockstep tick (20 per second — there is no collector-side decimation at all). The Python pipeline then derives two coarser clocks from that stream: position trails are downsampled to 1 Hz, and the damage timeline is bucketed into 10-second bins. So a 15-minute match yields ~18,000 ticks × 10 players = ~180,000 PlayerState samples on the wire, ~900 kept positioning samples per player, and ~90 timeline buckets. Details at stages 1 and 4.
1

In-game collection — statsgate.dll

statsgate/src/stat_client.cpp · C++ DLL hooked into BZCC's mission interface
Input
Live engine callbacks (Update, PreDamage, ObjectKilled, PreOrdnanceHit, PreSnipe, PrePickup)
→
Output
In-memory ClientStatSession protobuf (header + flat event stream)

The collector is a passive observer wedged between the engine and the mission script. record_update() fires once per lockstep turn and snapshots every rostered player's position, speed, health, ammo, current ODF, and T-key lock state. Combat events are callback-driven — one protobuf message per engine event, no sampling, no aggregation. This is the right architecture: collect at maximum fidelity, decimate downstream where the trade-offs are visible and reversible.

UpdateTick @ every turn (20/s)principled The rate isn't chosen by statsgate at all — GetLockstepTurn() advances at the engine's TPS (exu2::GetTPS(), observed 20 on every corpus match, stamped per-session as header.tick_rate). Recording every turn costs little (protobuf + gzip-9 crushes the repetition) and means no information is destroyed at the only stage where it would be unrecoverable. Any coarser cadence is a pipeline decision that can be revisited; this one can't be.
DAMAGE_TYPE_COLLISION / UNKNOWN droppedprincipled Collision spam carries no combat meaning; UNKNOWN is the engine's force-kill pathway that emits the notorious 228 sentinel values. Filtering at source is correct — but note the pipeline keeps a belt-and-braces sentinel filter anyway (stage 4) because older sessions in the corpus predate this collector filter.
BulletHit: player-vs-player onlydefensible AI-vs-AI hits are dropped collector-side to bound file size. The cost: thug_accuracy's PvE hit counts must come from the damage stream instead, and shots-vs-AI accuracy is reconstructed rather than observed. Acceptable, but it's a real asymmetry between the PvP and PvE legs of an axis that blends both.
Roster frozen at first tickquestionable The header's s64_to_nick map is built once; the source carries a literal // TODO: Rescan teamnums when players leave and join. A mid-match joiner is invisible to the entire downstream system — not low-rated, not excluded, simply absent. The pipeline's 75% presence gate (stage 5) handles the leaver half of this problem; the joiner half is unfixable downstream and belongs on the collector roadmap.
No match outcome recordedquestionable The header records shutdown_requested but not who won. Everything painful about validating this rating system (stage 10, and most of Part 2) traces back to this one missing field. The engine knows the outcome; the DLL is already hooked into PostRun. This is the cheapest expensive omission in the whole stack.

Transport & storage

last_tick() → gzip level 9 → data/sessions/<submitter>/<timestamp>.binpb.gz · git-synced
Input
In-memory session protobuf
→
Output
Immutable per-session archive file, organized by submitter

Sessions serialize once at mission end (gzip level 9, maximum compression — right call for write-once archives). Filenames are wall-clock timestamps for uniqueness; the map name lives inside the protobuf where it belongs. The pipeline's sync step mirrors new files additively — never deletes, never overwrites — which makes the raw corpus effectively append-only. This is archival discipline most telemetry systems never bother with: the original evidence is always re-processable, which is exactly what made five corpus-wide re-rates in ten weeks possible.

One file per session, per submitterprincipled Multiple players can record the same match; submitter-foldering keeps provenance and lets the cache key (submitter, source_file, size) deduplicate work. Duplicate-match reconciliation is handled at processing time rather than at capture time — correct, since capture-time dedup would destroy evidence.

4

Event aggregation — where the cadences are chosen

scripts/process_stats.py::process_match · one pass over the event stream
Input
~105–106 events per match
→
Output
Per-match blocks: leaderboard, timeline, positioning, kills, weapon meta, highlights…

One chronological sweep turns the raw stream into every per-match block the site (and the rating) consumes. The three clocks the user-visible data runs on are all set here, not in the game:

POSITIONING_SAMPLE_RATE_HZ = 1defensible Why 1 Hz and not 20, 10, or 0.5? Payload size is the binding constraint: trails ship to the browser as JSON (t/x/y/z/hp/ammo arrays per player). At 1 Hz a 15-min 10-player match is ~9,000 samples (~250 KB pretty-printed); at 20 Hz it's 180,000 (~5 MB) — unacceptable for a static site, and at 0.5 Hz the replay player's interpolation would visibly cut corners. For the consumers that matter it's adequate: heatmaps (64×64 grid) and distance-from-spawn metrics are insensitive to sub-second motion. The real casualty is path_length: BZ2 dogfighting is constant jinking, and 1 Hz chords systematically under-measure path length — by an amount that differs by playstyle (strafing brawler loses more than a straight-line traveler). Since path_length_per_sec is 20% of activity_score, which is the mobility axis, a measurement-bias gradient leaks into the rating. Small weight (0.08), real bias. Worth a one-off sensitivity check: reprocess five matches at 4 Hz and compare activity scores.
TIMELINE_BUCKET_SECONDS = 10principled Why 10 s? Pure UI artifact — it feeds the replay tab and momentum charts, never the rating. 10 s × 20 TPS = 200-tick bins, ~90 buckets per match: enough resolution to see a base trade, few enough datapoints that Chart.js animates smoothly with 10 player series. At 1 s you'd have 900 points × 10 series and a janky scrubber; at 60 s you'd erase the fight rhythm. Since no rating math touches it, the only criterion is legibility — and 10 s is right.
SENTINEL_DAMAGE_THRESHOLD = 1e6principled Targets the engine's DAMAGE_TYPE_UNKNOWN force-kill events (amount = 228 = 268,435,456) that legacy sessions still contain. Real per-event combat damage tops out in the low tens of thousands, so 1e6 sits ~100× above the legitimate maximum and ~268× below the artifact — a wide, safe corridor. The evidence chain (Ghidra decompile, struct layout, bit patterns) is documented in DATA_DICTIONARY §7. This is how magic numbers should be earned.
EFFECTIVE_KILL_WINDOW_SEC = 10, FINISHER_FLOOR = 0.30defensible Kill credit is distributed by damage share over the 10 s before the kill, with 30% guaranteed to the finisher. The mechanism is excellent (it de-noises kill-stealing at the source, and Σ effective = Σ raw keeps lobby totals invariant). The parameters are gut-tuned: why 10 s and not a TTK-derived window? Why 30% and not 20%? Neither has been sensitivity-tested. Fine to ship; should be on the sweep list since thug_kill_rate carries 0.20 weight.
Pilot-victim & self-damage carve-outsprincipled v2.9 stops kill/death credit for shooting ejected pilots (victim-only gate, so a pilot who kills a ship keeps credit); v2.6 stops Blink self-splash from inflating PvP buckets. Both are anti-farming corrections with precise, documented blast radii. This is the measurement layer earning its A.
Spawn = median of first 3 samplesdefensible Robust to one teleport-y first frame; 3 is the smallest count where a median means anything. At 1 Hz this is a 3-second window — players rarely travel far in it. Cheap and adequate; 5 would be marginally safer for laggy starts.
activity_score = 100·(0.5·(1−base%) + 0.3·max-dist + 0.2·path-rate)questionable The weights (0.5/0.3/0.2) are unaudited, and the two distance terms are normalized by match-relative p95 — which means mobility is a relative score inside a relative score (z-scored again at stage 7). Double relativization makes cross-match interpretation slippery, and the 1 Hz path-length bias above feeds the 0.2 term. The axis only carries 0.08 weight, so the damage is bounded — but this is the least-principled formula that touches the rating.

Row gates — who gets rated at all

scripts/process_stats.py (flags) → scripts/elo.py (pure omission)
Input
Per-player leaderboard rows
→
Output
The rated lobby: rows minus campod / low-activity (15 + 7 rows excluded corpus-wide)

Excluded rows are omitted, not penalized — no delta, no matches-played bump, as if the match never happened for that player. The philosophy is correct (the prior reviewer and I agree here; see Part 2). The implementation has a shape problem: both gates are cliffs.

CAMPOD_MAX_SHARE = 0.25defensible Why 25%? No documented derivation — it's a "feels like spectating" threshold. The gate is binary: 24% campod time = fully rated (including the idle quarter dragging your per-minute axes), 26% = invisible. A proportional alternative — weight the row's lobby contribution by non-campod share — removes the cliff and the incentive question ("I'm having a bad match; if I pod for 5 more minutes it never happened").
LOW_ACTIVITY_MIN_PRESENCE = 0.75defensible Presence window (first→last event tick) must cover 75% of the match. One clean window check catches both late joins and mid-match DCs, and deliberately does not punish dead time — good design. Same cliff critique: a 74% presence player is erased, a 76% one is fully scored against per-minute denominators they only partially played. The per-minute axes use match minutes, not present minutes, so partial-presence players who clear the gate are mildly under-measured — a second-order bug worth one line: use presence-window minutes in thug_kill_rate.
ELO_MIN_PLAYER_COUNT = 6, MIN_DURATION = 240 sprincipled Below 3v3 the lobby z-score (stage 7) is statistically meaningless, and a sub-4-minute match is a remake. 13 + 5 matches excluded corpus-wide. Sensible floors; if anything, 6 is generous — z-scores on n=6 are still wild (see stage 7).

Input
All stage-4 aggregates for one player
→
Output

Each row carries the full attribution lattice: dealt = pvp + pve + self (invariant-checked), effective PvP kills, per-weapon shots/hits with PvP splits, activity_score, target_lock_pct, at_base_pilot_sec, and the three boolean gates. The eight-axis composite — Pi

scripts/elo.py::compute_performance_index
Input
Rated lobby rows
→
Output
Pi ∈ [−1, +1] per player + per-axis attribution

Each axis produces one raw number per player, gets z-scored within the lobby, clipped to ±2σ, divided by 2, then weight-summed:

Missing axes (no positioning data, nobody sniped) self-omit and their weight redistributes pro-rata — graceful degradation done right.

Lobby z-score on n = 6–10questionable This is the load-bearing statistical decision nobody has interrogated — every prior critique attacked the update math instead. A population z-score over 8 observations is extremely noisy: σ estimated from n=8 has a standard error of ~25% of itself, one outlier owns the denominator, and clipping then bites on noise rather than on outliers. The same absolute performance scores wildly differently depending on who else showed up — which is partially intended (relative-to-lobby is the point) but the noise component isn't. Rank-based scoring (percentile within lobby, mapped to [−1,1]) preserves the relative framing with far better small-n behavior, kills the clip constant entirely, and is a one-afternoon forensic alt-mode. See improvement #4.
clip(z, −2, +2) / 2defensible Outlier control plus range normalization in one move. ±2σ would be a principled choice against a stable σ; against an n=8 sample σ it's a damage limiter for the noise problem above. The /2 mapping to [−1,1] is then purely cosmetic (matches Ei's range). If the z-step were replaced by ranks, this constant evaporates — a sign it's patching rather than designing.
THUG_WEIGHTS (0.20/0.20/0.16/0.15/0.12/0.08/0.005/0.005)defensible Hand-tuned, but the Dirichlet perturbation test (ρ 0.982 under jittered weights) proves the leaderboard isn't knife-edged on them, and the v2.10 de-weight of the two "luxury" axes to 0.005 was correctly evidence-driven (ablation showed them inert; z-scoring punished non-participation). Honest detail: the raw dict now sums to 0.92 and runtime renormalization silently uplifts the six core axes ~8.7% — documented, slightly inelegant, harmless.
ALPHA_PVE = 0.5questionable PvE work counts at exactly half a PvP equivalent in three axes. Why 0.5? Round-number prior, never swept. It sits in the two heaviest axes (thug_kill_rate, thug_efficiency), so it shapes farm-vs-fight incentives more than any other single scalar. A {0.25, 0.5, 0.75} forensic sweep is cheap and overdue.
snipe cap: min(snipes/5, 1) pre-zprincipled Capping before the z-score so one 9-snipe outlier can't deform the lobby distribution is exactly the right order of operations. (At 0.005 weight the axis barely matters now, but the technique deserves the nod.)

Role & tier adjustments — the epicycle layer

scripts/elo.py · v2.4 commander shift + v2.8 low-tier lift
Input
Post-clip per-axis z-scores
→
Output
Shifted z-scores for commander rows + eligible low-tier rows

Two surgical fairness patches, both applied in post-clip space so non-targeted players are byte-identical. Commander rows get per-axis shifts toward a "typical commander" baseline (audit-derived priors with shrinkage, two hand-locked); established low-tier thugs get their kill rate recomputed over ship-available time. Individually each is careful. Collectively they are the visible part of a complexity-budget problem (Part 2, finding 7).

COMMANDER_BASELINE_SHRINKAGE = 30principled Standard pseudo-observation shrinkage. With 214 commander rows banked, live data now carries 214/(214+30) = 88% of the blend — the seed priors have already gracefully retired for the four unlocked axes. The mechanism worked exactly as designed; this is how to ship a prior.
Locked pve_share prior = −0.05 (reward)questionable Design intent: actively reward commanders for PvE work. But the empirical commander mean on this axis is +0.0489 — commanders already out-PvE the lobby naturally. The lock therefore grants a bonus on top of an axis they already win: ~0.10 of post-clip z versus the descriptive baseline. The cost is tiny (the prior ablation measured ≤3.9 ELO), so keep it if you want — but the documented rationale ("reward the work they should do") no longer matches what the lock does ("double-pay work they demonstrably already do"). Re-justify or retire the rationale.
Locked target_lock prior = −0.10 vs empirical −0.391defensible A deliberate partial pardon: cover ~25% of the real commander T-key shortfall, keep ~75% as pressure. That's a coherent normative stance, clearly documented, with measured sub-noise cost. I disagree with critics who called this corruption — and note the axis now carries 0.005 weight, so the whole debate is about ≤0.001 of Pi. Functionally moot.
LOWTIER_LIFT_CUTOFF = 1460, TAPER = 60questionable Two arbitrary constants defining who counts as "low-tier" — and at the current distribution they capture 10 of 35 players (29% of the league), including everyone down to 29 points below the anchor. That's not a low-tier safety net; that's a below-median adjustment program. The two-pass design (eligibility keyed on canonical no-lift ratings) is genuinely clever — the gate can't self-sustain — but the population it covers says the cutoff drifted from intent. Either drop the cutoff to ~1400 or rename what this is.

The ELO update — K, E, and the asymmetries

scripts/elo.py::_rating_pass · chronological, two-pass when the lift is active
Input

→
Output

Expected performance compares you to the median opponent; you gain when you beat expectation, lose (dampened) when you miss it. Every constant below is doing something — and three of them quietly make the system non-zero-sum.

median(opponents) referenceprincipled The strongest empirical result in the project's record: the Phase 2C full re-rate under hard-MAX collapsed predictive ρ from 0.462 to 0.188 and inflated the mean rating +522 above anchor (Ei saturates near −1 for every sub-max player, so average play prints rating forever). Median survived its trial by ordeal. I fully concur with the prior reviewer here — and would add that the experiment design (forensic alt-mode, then decide) is the template for everything else on my improvement list.
ELO_LOGISTIC_SCALE = 800defensible Why 800 when classic Elo uses 400? Doubling the scale halves the steepness: a +200 rating edge expects only +0.28 composite (classic 400 would demand +0.52-equivalent). Deliberately flat for a small league where rating gaps are noisy — defensible. But note what the fresh calibration table shows: residuals are near-zero across all ten gap buckets (MAE 0.017), which mostly tells you the corpus only spans gaps of about −176 to +254 with the bulk inside ±100. The curve is calibrated where there's data; its steepness has never been tested where it would matter. 800 is a prior wearing a calibration certificate.
K: 40·(1−n/(n+10)) + 12, + inactivity boostprincipled Rookie K≈52 → n=10 K=32 → settles at 12+. A clean hyperbolic decay with sane endpoints, plus the Phase 2B additive boost (+0.05/day, cap +20) that buys most of Glicko-2's time-decay benefit at trivial cost (audit: max single-row boost on this dense corpus was +1.3). I concur with shipping it and with deferring Glicko-2 — the corpus is too dense to need RD machinery.
ELO_RATING_SCALE = 2.5defensible Pure unit conversion from composite-space to rating points: max theoretical swing K·2.5·2 = 260 for a rookie, ~15–40 typical. Any value works if everything downstream agrees; 2.5 produces a leaderboard spread (1416–1742) that reads naturally against the 1500 anchor. Fine.
Loss aversion 0.85 + floor 1000 / taper 150questionable Every loss is multiplied by 0.85; losses near the floor approach zero. Result, measured on this corpus from elo_history.json: +6,276 ELO granted, −4,944 deducted — net +1,331 ELO created in 107 matches (≈+12.4/match; mean rating 1538 = anchor + exactly the created amount / 35 players). That's a 21% subsidy on the loss side of the ledger, by design, for retention psychology. Defensible for a display rating; corrosive for anything downstream that treats VTSR-T as a skill estimate (Team Balonce does). You don't need the α>0 blend to measure this — the chart in Part 2 is the measurement. The dual-track question (display vs pure) is real and currently unanswered.
Two-pass lift with canonical-anchored Eiprincipled Pass 1 computes no-lift ratings; pass 2 re-rates with the lift, anchoring opponent references to the canonical snapshot so non-eligible players are byte-identical and eligible players self-damp. Whatever I think of the lift's cutoff (stage 8), the isolation engineering is impeccable.

Part 2

The methodology critique

Findings ordered by severity. Where the prior reviewer (the v3 retrospective and its decision memos) took a position, each card carries an explicit CONCUR / DISSENT / EXTEND strip. Everything below cites either source code or the fresh validator run executed for this report (2026-06-10, 107 rated matches, schema 10).

Fundamental

1 · VTSR-T does not predict match outcomes — and the trend is the wrong direction

On the fresh run, the team with higher mean VTSR-T wins a clean-win match 37.5% of the time (12/32, CI 22.9–54.7) — below coin-flip, and down from 43.3% at n=30 and 45.2% at n=31. Even the best post-hoc aggregation (hard MAX) manages only 50.0%. Log-loss (0.707) is worse than always guessing 50/50 (0.693).

The standard defense — "all clean-wins are tight-gap lobbies, the rating's ceiling is untestable" — is true but incomplete. The fresh gap breakout undermines it: the small-gap bucket [0,25) predicts at 47.1% (fine — genuinely a coin flip), but the mid-gap bucket [25,100), n=15, predicts at 26.7% — the rating is inverted exactly where its signal should begin to emerge. n=15 is small and the CI is wide (10.9–52.0), so I won't claim the inversion is established. I will claim that "untested" is no longer the honest summary; "untested, with the early returns pointing the wrong way" is.

Extend Prior reviewer said the lopsided-prediction ceiling is untestable on this corpus (v3 §13.6) and parked it. I concur it's untestable at the top end — but the mid-gap inversion is new signal from the fresh run that the v3 snapshot didn't have, and it converts a passive "wait for data" item into an active "watch this number every run" item.

Fundamental

2 · The synthetic-winner proxy at 100% is circularity, not validation

The proxy declares the team with higher mean Pi the winner and checks agreement with the kill-feed-inferred clean_win. Fresh run: 32/32 = 100% agreement (was 93.3%, then 90.3%). The prior reviewer celebrated crossing the 85% bar as "the single most important methodological contribution of Phase 1… unlocks α>0 validation."

Look at what both sides of the comparison are made of. A clean win means you razed the enemy base — recyclers and factories destroyed. Pi's heaviest axes are net damage share, kill rate, and PvE share — which is literally damage to structures. The winning team's players mechanically accumulate the quantities the proxy averages. Perfect agreement doesn't mean Pi independently identifies skill that wins matches; it means Pi and the winner label are two functions of the same events. And the trajectory — 93% → 90% → 100% as the corpus grew — is what convergence-to-determinism looks like, not what independent-measurement noise looks like.

The consequence is serious: the planned α>0 pilot (v3 §13.2) would blend "winner" outcomes that are a deterministic echo of Pi back into a Pi-driven rating. That's not adding outcome information; it's re-weighting the composite toward its own structure-damage axes and calling it win/loss data. The sweep would "work" — metrics would move smoothly — and validate nothing.

Dissent Prior reviewer: the proxy "unlocks α>0 validation against the full corpus" (v3 §5.5, F.1). I dissent on the inference, not the measurement. The 85% threshold tested agreement, but agreement was never the risk — circularity was, and 100% agreement is the circularity diagnostic firing. α>0 needs ground truth that is causally upstream of Pi's inputs, i.e. the actual game result recorded by the engine. See improvements #1 and #3.
Needs work

3 · Winner inference reaches 31% of the corpus; the fix is one proto field

The toggle-model winner inference (recycler/factory destruction order) yields clean_win for 32 of 107 rated matches, contested for 1, and unclear for 74. Every outcome-anchored question this project wants to ask — does rating predict winners, is the loss subsidy distorting, what should α be — is being answered on a 30% subsample that is additionally biased toward decisive stomps (the matches where bases actually fell).

The engine knows who won. The collector is already hooked into PostRun and already writes shutdown_requested into the header. A winner_team field (plus a finished_normally flag) is a one-evening change to statsgate + proto + pipeline, and it converts every future session into ground truth. There is no analysis investment anywhere in this system with remotely comparable return.

Extend Prior reviewer listed "manual winner attestation" as the active option (v3 §13.6) and treated corpus growth as the passive one. Both are inferior to instrumenting the source — attestation doesn't scale and decays in accuracy; corpus growth without the field grows the unclear pile at the same 70% rate.
Needs work

4 · Headline metrics are drifting down as the corpus grows — and nobody is watching

Across the three validator runs on record: Spearman ρ 0.462 → 0.468 → 0.445; clean-win accuracy 43.3 → 45.2 → 37.5%; bootstrap noise σ 27.0 → 29.4 → 31.9 ELO. The v3 doc's own growth hypothesis (§13.3) predicted the opposite: "ρ increases, σ decreases" as data accumulates.

Three candidate explanations, none currently distinguishable: (a) sampling noise — plausible, the deltas are within run-to-run wobble; (b) the v2.9/v2.10 re-rates genuinely traded predictive power for fairness — the pilot-kill exclusion and the snipe/T-key de-weight both removed signal along with noise; (c) the league's composition is shifting (new players, role churn) faster than the rating adapts. The fact that I can't tell you which is the finding: the validator runs ad-hoc, results aren't time-series'd, and the v2.10 re-rate shipped without a before/after validator diff. The project has the best validation tooling in its class and uses it episodically.

Extend Prior reviewer proposed growth tracking as a "READY NOW, recurring, ~half day" item (v3 §13.3) but ranked it third among next steps. I promote it to the top of the do-now list (after the winner field): the drift is already here, and every future re-rate decision is unanchored without per-run metric history.

Needs work

5 · Per-lobby z-scoring on 6–10 players is the unexamined statistical weak point

Every axis is standardized against the lobby's own mean and standard deviation — estimated from as few as six observations, with the rated player included in their own baseline. At n=8, the sample σ carries ~25% relative error; a single outlier (one AFK-ish row, one pop-off game) reshapes everyone's z-scores; the ±2σ clip then triggers on estimation noise as often as on true outliers. The downstream K-factor and logistic machinery is built on top of per-match scores whose noise floor is set here.

Rank-based scoring — map each player's within-lobby rank to a fixed grid on [−1,1] (or normal scores) — keeps the relative-to-lobby semantics, is distribution-free, immune to outliers, needs no clip constant, and behaves identically for n=6 and n=10. The cost: it discards magnitude (a narrow win over the #2 player scores the same as a blowout). That trade should be tested, not assumed either way — it's a textbook forensic alt-mode, and the validator can referee it with zero new metrics.

Extend Neither the v1/v2 critiques nor v3 ever questioned the z-step — all artillery was aimed at the update rule (median/MAX, K, priors). This is the gap in the prior record: the noisiest stage in the whole chain is the one that's never had an experiment.
Minor

6 · The rating economy mints ~12 ELO per match by design — measured, not hypothesized

Summing every delta in elo_history.json: +6,275.5 granted, −4,944.2 deducted, net +1,331.3 ELO created across 107 rated matches. Mean rating now 1538 — the anchor plus exactly the minted amount spread over 35 players. The sources are the 0.85 loss multiplier (a 15% rebate on every loss) and the floor taper (losses vanish entirely near 1000).

This is not a bug — it's documented retention psychology, and at +12.4/match the drift is gentle (compare hard-MAX's +522 pathology). But two consumers currently treat VTSR-T as an unbiased skill estimate: Team Balonce and the tier badges. A participation subsidy of ~38 ELO/player accumulated in 8 weeks will, over a year, move players a full tier on attendance alone. The honest framing is CS2's: this is a display rating; a zero-sum shadow track is what balancing should read.

Concur Prior reviewer: "mathematically real but empirically unmeasured… gated on α>0" (v3 §C.3, F.2). I concur the dual-track decision is open — but the measurement itself was never gated: the anchor-sum audit above took one loop over the history file. The number is +12.4 ELO/match. It no longer needs to be called unmeasured.

Minor

7 · The complexity budget is overdrawn: six adjustment mechanisms, ~25 constants, zero outcome regularization

Count the moving parts bolted onto the core update: commander axis-shift (6 priors + shrinkage + lock set), low-tier lift (4 constants, two-pass), two exclusion gates (2 thresholds), loss aversion + floor taper (3 constants), inactivity K-boost (2), effective-kill crediting (3), ALPHA_PVE, the α-blend stub. Each shipped with care, documentation, and usually an experiment. But they were each validated against the others held fixed, on a corpus of ~100 matches, with no outcome ground truth to regularize against — the classic recipe for a system that fits its designers' intuitions about fairness rather than a measurable target.

The tell: 10 of 35 players qualify for the "low-tier" lift, the commander baseline has live data 7× heavier than its priors, two locked priors now contradict their own documented rationale (finding in stage 8), and the two de-weighted axes exist at 0.005 purely as UI preview. Pruning candidates are accumulating. A system this size needs a standing rule: every mechanism must name the validator metric that would justify its removal — v3 §14 started this with re-tuning triggers; extend it to existence triggers.

Extend v3 §14's calibration-protocol table is the right instinct (each lever has a revisit trigger). Extension: triggers fire only if someone is watching — which loops back to finding 4's per-run tracking. A trigger table without a time-series is a smoke alarm without a battery.
Solid

8 · What is right — and should be defended against future "improvements"

  • The composite measures something real. Split-half self-consistency ρ = 0.814 across 23 players. That is a strong persistent-skill signal — the axes are not noise, and any future redesign must clear this bar, not just match it.
  • Median opponent reference. Phase 2C's negative result is decisive and correctly interpreted. Anyone proposing MAX/softmax for the update rule (as opposed to lobby-time team aggregation) should be pointed at the +522 drift and sent home.
  • Pure-omission gates. Excluding campod/partial rows with zero penalty (rather than zero-scoring them) protects both the excluded player and the lobby baseline. Right philosophy; only the cliff shape needs work (stage 5).
  • Calibration where data exists. MAE 0.017 across ten gap buckets — within its observed range, the E-curve tracks reality. The caveat (finding at stage 9) is about extrapolation, not about the fit.
  • Forensic alt-mode culture. Every contested mechanism ships as a parallel JSON pair scored by the same validator. This is the experimental pattern the improvements below all reuse — it's the project's best invention after the composite itself.
  • Engineering honesty. Constants echoed into outputs, per-delta axis attribution, reconcile views, decision memos with refuted hypotheses left visible. Rare and valuable.
Concur This list substantially matches the prior reviewer's F.1 ("where I am confident"). The agreement is genuine — where the v3 author claims the data validates the design, the data does. Our divergence is almost entirely about what the synthetic-winner proxy licenses (finding 2) and how alarming the outcome-prediction picture is (finding 1).

Part 4

Everything else I noticed

Observations that didn't fit the severity ladder — engineering, process, and a few footnotes for future maintainers.

  • The dual-descriptor decode is exemplary. Byte-parity discipline for v1 sessions, schema stamped as telemetry-only, one normalized internal shape. When the winner field (improvement #1) bumps the proto, this machinery is why it'll be painless.
  • The bootstrap σ is a lower bound, not the truth. The resampling proxy (Σ preserved deltas) skips the sequential Rbefore chain, so loss-aversion and floor-taper effects stay baked into each delta. True re-rating per resample would widen the band. Treat ±32 as optimistic — which strengthens, not weakens, improvement #6.
  • Ablation and Dirichlet rank by career-mean Pi, not full re-rates. Documented Phase 1 shortcut, fine — but worth promoting to true re-rates before any weight decision is ever made off them, since the proxy ignores path dependence entirely.
  • peak_vtsr has been invalidated five times in ten weeks (v2.6, v2.8, schema-13, v2.9, v2.10). Internally fine — the corpus re-rates cleanly — but the public leaderboard's "Peak" column silently changes meaning each time. Consider epoching: "peak (era 10)" or a re-rate changelog link on the methodology modal.
  • The K inactivity boost is well-built and currently decorative — max single-row effect +1.3 ELO on this dense corpus. Good: it was shipped for the future, audited as null for the present, and documented as such. This is the right way to ship a dormant mechanism.
  • statsgate's roster TODO matters more than it looks. "Rescan teamnums when players leave and join" — until fixed, mid-match joiners are unrecorded entirely (not even excludable), and their damage against recorded players still flows into the stream attributed to team-only context. Low frequency, but it quietly violates the "every player in the lobby is in the header" assumption several aggregators make.
  • Validator seed is fixed (12345). Good for reproducibility; bad if nobody ever varies it — bootstrap CI width itself has run-to-run noise that a fixed seed hides. Vary the seed across the per-run time-series (improvement #2) for free.
  • Documentation drift risk is real but managed. The rules files annotate every known duplication (title-resolver quadruplication, tier-table mirrors). The ELO section of the always-applied rules is now ~40% of the file and growing per version; consider moving the per-version changelog into DEVELOPER_GUIDE §13 and keeping only current-state in rules.
  • This report's own provenance: the validator was re-run fresh for this analysis (2026-06-10) because the on-disk report predated the v2.10 re-rate; all numbers cited here are from that run or computed directly from the canonical JSON outputs. The snapshot baked into this page is reproducible via fable/tools/build_report_data.py.
